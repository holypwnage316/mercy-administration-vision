Ministry Pathways and Administrative Care

A proposal for helping people at Mercy move more naturally from visiting, to belonging, to serving, to growing.

Tithely is considered here as one possible tool to support that ministry work.

1

Summary

💡The big idea

Mercy has an opportunity to strengthen the way people are welcomed, followed up with, communicated with, and connected into the life of the church. Some of this work is in-person hospitality. Some of it is administrative structure. Together, they form a ministry pathway.

The goal is not to buy more tools. The goal is to build clearer ministry pathways, and then use Tithely thoughtfully where it can support those pathways.
👋 Visiting Welcome and orient
→
🤝 Belonging Follow up and connect

2

Why it matters

🧭The ministry problem this addresses

Guests, members, leaders, and volunteers often need different kinds of care and communication. Without a clear shared system, important follow-up can depend on memory, scattered messages, hallway conversations, or individual leaders carrying too much in their heads.

A clearer pathway would help Mercy become more consistent without becoming impersonal. The aim is not to automate care, but to make sure care is less likely to fall through the cracks.

Ministry opportunity

👋Greeter ministry

Greeters would welcome people as they enter the church, with special attention given to first-time guests. A greeter could introduce themselves, answer basic questions, and help guests know where to find important areas like restrooms, children’s spaces, and the hospitality or snack area.

Result: guests feel seen, welcomed, and less uncertain when they arrive.

How Tithely could support this

Greeters would still do the personal work of welcome. Tithely could support the next step by helping Mercy capture guest information through forms and connect that information to people records and follow-up workflows.

Ministry opportunity

🚗Parking lot attendants

Parking lot attendants could help direct cars, improve traffic flow, and act as a simple drop-off support for people who need to get closer to the entrance. This could especially serve guests, elderly members, families with young children, or anyone with mobility challenges.

Result: Mercy communicates care before someone even enters the building.

How Tithely could support this

This role is primarily an in-person hospitality need. Tithely could still help with volunteer organization by keeping team members grouped, scheduled, and reachable for reminders.

Tithely Church Management Software

Ministry opportunity

💌First-time guest experience

A first-time guest could fill out a connection card, which is then entered into the church database and added to a first-time guest workflow. On Monday, the guest could receive an email thanking them for visiting, briefly explaining Mercy’s ministries, and encouraging them to return or get connected further.

Result: guest follow-up becomes warm, consistent, and repeatable.

How Tithely could support this

Tithely People describes member profiles, forms, registrations, follow-ups, automations, and targeted messaging. Those pieces could support a first-time guest workflow from connection card to Monday email to any additional follow-up.

Tithely People
6

Administrative improvement

🗂️Church and people database

A stronger church database could help Mercy organize people by age, role, membership status, ministry involvement, or custom criteria. It could also support workflows for first-time guests, member birthdays, volunteer follow-up, and other recurring care needs.

This would also make targeted communication easier, such as sending immediate or scheduled emails and text messages to specific people or groups.

Result: leaders have a clearer shared picture of people, needs, and next steps.

How Tithely could support this

Tithely People is the better fit for this need. It describes storing member profiles and contact details, syncing giving and people data, using tags and smart groups, tracking attendance, collecting information through forms, and supporting follow-ups and automations. Tithely Messaging also connects to this need by allowing messages to be sent to specific roles, groups, or the church as a whole.

Administrative improvement

💬Communication and groups

Mercy currently communicates primarily through email. Email is still useful for formal announcements, but it can be weak for ongoing ministry coordination. Threads get lost, people reply to different versions of the same conversation, and context becomes hard to keep together.

Mercy also currently uses Discord, which helps some people, but it has not become a dependable communication home for everyone. If many people are not using it, or only some ministries are active there, important communication still ends up split across email, Discord, texts, and informal conversations.

A group-based communication tool could give staff, ministry leaders, the worship team, volunteers, ministry groups, and other teams a shared place for their conversations. Instead of hunting through inboxes, each ministry team could keep messages, reminders, questions, updates, and replies connected to that group.

This would also help with time-sensitive communication, such as weather cancellations, location changes, volunteer reminders, and ministry-specific encouragements.

Result: fewer scattered email threads, less dependence on a tool many people do not use, clearer context, and better connection between Sundays.

How Tithely could support this

Tithely Groups is designed for church groups and ministry teams. Tithely describes group channels, direct messages, two-way push notifications, group rosters, attendance, and the ability for leaders to message their own groups without waiting on the church office.

Tithely Messaging still matters for broader communication because it supports email, text messaging, group targeting, scheduled messages, and church-wide updates.

Administrative improvement

🎵Worship and service planning

A worship and service planning process could help organize songs, sermon notes, transitions, readings, volunteer assignments, and service outlines. Leaders could schedule worship leaders and volunteers, send reminders, and make sure everyone knows what is happening before Sunday morning.

Result: worship and service teams prepare with less friction and more clarity.

How Tithely could support this

Tithely Service Planning is focused on organizing worship services, scheduling worship leaders and volunteers, sending automatic reminders, and sharing service outlines. It also mentions setlists, a song library, rehearsal resources, and SongSelect integration for worship teams.

Platform connection

🔗How Tithely fits

Tithely should be evaluated by how well it supports Mercy’s ministry pathway, not just by how many features it lists. The most useful features are the ones that make welcome, follow-up, communication, giving, groups, and service planning easier to sustain.

Welcome and guests Forms, people records, automation, workflows, email and text follow-up.
Communication Group channels, direct messages, push notifications, email, text, scheduled messages, and urgent updates.
Belonging Searchable groups, ministry teams, rosters, attendance, group messaging, and group discovery through the church app.
Generosity Digital giving, recurring giving, donor records, statements, and analytics.
Sunday worship Service outlines, song planning, worship team scheduling, volunteer reminders, setlists, and rehearsal resources.

Side benefit: a Mercy church app

If Mercy used Tithely more broadly, one secondary benefit could be a branded church app where people can find messages, events, sermons, giving, ministry groups, and push notifications in one place. This does not need to drive the whole proposal, but it could support the broader goal of keeping people connected during the week.

Suggested next steps

✅A reasonable starting point

  1. Present these ideas to the deaconate and/or session for discussion, especially where ministry needs and administrative structure overlap.
  2. Trial Tithely free for 30 days so Mercy can evaluate the tools in practice rather than only in theory.
  3. Implement the pieces that seem to offer the most value first, then decide what should be expanded, adjusted, or set aside.
