Core vision

Better systems in service of better care.

Mercy has an opportunity to make ministry more welcoming, connected, and organized by strengthening both our in-person hospitality and our behind-the-scenes administrative systems.

The goal is not simply to add programs or buy technology. The goal is to build clearer ministry pathways, then use tools like Tithely or Subsplash to support connection, care, service, and discipleship.

Ministry opportunities

Five areas to discuss

These are practical opportunities Mercy could pursue in stages. Use the filters to focus the conversation.

Greeter Ministry

Welcome people as they enter, introduce first-time guests to someone by name, and help them find restrooms, children’s areas, and hospitality.

Helps guests feel seen, welcomed, and less uncertain.

Parking Lot Attendants

Direct cars, improve traffic flow, and assist anyone who needs to be dropped off closer to the entrance.

Communicates care before someone enters the building.

First-Time Guest Follow-Up

Use a connection card, add guests to a workflow, send a Monday thank-you email, explain Mercy’s ministries, and invite a next step.

Makes guest care more consistent and less dependent on memory.

Church People Database

Organize people by age, role, membership, ministry involvement, or custom groups. Create workflows for guests, birthdays, members, volunteers, and pastoral care.

Gives leaders a shared place to care for people well.

Messaging and Groups

Create groups for staff, ministry leaders, volunteers, worship team, small groups, and other teams. Send reminders, encouragement, and timely updates.

Reduces scattered communication and connects people between Sundays.

Worship and Service Planning

Plan songs, sermon notes, transitions, readings, and service elements. Schedule volunteers and share service outlines.

Helps Sunday service teams prepare with clarity.

Product connection

How Tithely and Subsplash connect to the vision

This is the bridge between ministry needs and platform value. The question is not “Which feature sounds nice?” but “Which tool helps Mercy care for people more clearly?”

Conversation starter

A simple way to say it

The goal is not to buy more tools. The goal is to build clearer ministry pathways, and then use the tools we already have or are considering to support those pathways.

Better umbrella phrase

Ministry Pathways and Administrative Care

This language keeps the people-centered vision in front while still naming the practical systems needed to support it.

It sounds less like “admin cleanup” and more like a ministry strategy for helping people be welcomed, known, connected, and equipped.
